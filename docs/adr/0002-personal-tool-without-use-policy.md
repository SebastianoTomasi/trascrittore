# A personal tool without a use policy

The transcriber is a personal, general-purpose tool that people close to the
maintainer install on their own PCs. It is not a work tool and assumes nothing
about what it is used for, so it imposes no consent step or other use policy: it
recognizes whoever has voice clips in the voice library of the PC where it runs,
and whoever uses it answers for how they use it.

## Considered options

- **A consent rule**, under which only the voices of people who agreed may
  enter a library and a withdrawal removes them: rejected because the
  transcriber cannot check it, and a personal tool leaves that judgement to its
  user.
- **A work tool bound by an employer's obligations**, such as consent records
  and an impact assessment: rejected because the transcriber is not offered as
  a work tool.

## Consequences

- Nothing in the transcriber asks whether the people recorded or recognized
  agreed. Someone who adopts it for work takes on the obligations of that use.
- Offering it as a work tool, or to people beyond those close to the
  maintainer, reopens this decision.
